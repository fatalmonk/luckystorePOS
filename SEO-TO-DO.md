### <a id="seo-012-openai-crawler-guidance-2026-10-24"></a>SEO-012 OpenAI crawler guidance — 2026-10-24

**Official guidance observed:** OpenAI's [Overview of OpenAI Crawlers](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots), accessed 2026-10-24, says OAI-SearchBot is used to surface websites in ChatGPT Search and recommends allowing it in `robots.txt`; opting it out means sites will not appear in ChatGPT Search answers, though they may still appear as navigational links. The same page distinguishes OAI-SearchBot from GPTBot, whose robots directive governs crawling for model training. OpenAI says its systems may take about 24 hours to adjust after a robots.txt update.

**Current local rule interpretation:** In `apps/customer_storefront/public/robots.txt`, OAI-SearchBot has no dedicated group, so the existing `User-agent: *` group applies. That group allows `/` and disallows `/api/`, account/checkout/cart/order/login/signup paths, wishlist, search, preview, and admin. The SEO-011 local matcher already tested OAI-SearchBot and observed the same outcome: public pages allowed; the listed private/API paths blocked. Therefore, the current file does allow OAI-SearchBot to crawl public pages under the wildcard, consistent with OpenAI's recommendation; the absence of a dedicated group does not itself establish an access block. Adding a dedicated group is not required for access and could change path coverage if its exclusions were not kept aligned.

**Limit and disposition:** This establishes only the checked-in robots permission; it does not establish live-host parity, firewall/IP-range reachability, or successful crawler fetches. The clarity check is complete; no robots change or publication was made. Live-host `robots.txt` parity remains unverified under SEO-001 and is not established by this note.

### <a id="seo-013-google-extended-versus-googlebot-2026-10-24"></a>SEO-013 Google-Extended versus Googlebot — 2026-10-24

**Official guidance observed:** Google's [common crawlers reference](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawlers-fetchers/google-common-crawlers), accessed 2026-10-24 and last updated 2026-10-15, says Google-Extended is a standalone `robots.txt` product token, not a separate HTTP request user agent. Its control governs whether crawled content may be used for Gemini model training and grounding in Gemini Apps and Vertex AI; Google says it does not affect inclusion in Google Search and is not a Search ranking signal. Google's [AI features guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features), accessed 2026-10-24, says Googlebot directives control how sites are crawled for Search, including Search AI features; it separately points site owners to Google-Extended for certain other Google systems.

There is no named `Googlebot` group, so Googlebot uses `User-agent: *`, whose current public/private path outcome was tested in the SEO-011 local matrix. Thus the file expresses a distinct Google-Extended content-use control while public Search crawling is governed separately by wildcard rules for Googlebot; the robots text alone does not establish actual crawling, indexing, serving, or Search AI appearance.

**Disposition and limits:** The task's distinction is documented; no robots change or publication was made. This is checked-in source evidence only. Live-host parity and Google crawl/indexing behavior remain unverified under SEO-001 and related verification tasks.
